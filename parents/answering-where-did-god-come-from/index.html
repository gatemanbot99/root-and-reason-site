A practical guide

How to answer when your kid asks where God came from

You do not need a philosophy degree. You need one clear truth, a little patience, and permission to say that wonder is part of faith.

Start with the short answer

Say, “Everything God made had a beginning, but God did not. He has always been God.” Then read Psalm 90:2 together: “from everlasting to everlasting you are God.” For a younger child, that may be enough. Do not bury a good answer under ten minutes of explanation.

A child asking this question is not being disrespectful. They are applying a rule they know: things come from somewhere. Affirm the thinking behind the question. You might say, “That is a smart question. Almost everything we know has a beginning, so it feels strange to think about Someone who does not.”

Make the key distinction

Explain that God is not one more object inside the universe. He made the universe, including time itself. Asking what happened “before” time is a little like asking what is north of the North Pole. Our ordinary categories have reached their edge.

Older children may ask, “Then why can God exist without a maker?” The answer is not that Christians make a special exception. It is that something must be eternal and independent, or nothing could ever begin. Scripture identifies that eternal reality not as a blind force, but as the living God who says, “I AM WHO I AM” in Exodus 3:14.

Keep Jesus in the answer

Do not accidentally speak as though Jesus began at Christmas. John 1:1-3 teaches that the Word was with God, was God, and made all things. The eternal Son took on a human nature and was born in Bethlehem. He is fully God and fully human. This keeps a simple answer connected to the core of Christian faith.

Let mystery stay mysterious

“I do not fully understand how God can have no beginning” is an honest sentence. Mystery is not the same as contradiction. We can know something truly without knowing it completely. A child knows the ocean is real without holding all of it in a bucket.

End with reassurance, not abstraction. Because God has always been, He will never stop being. His promises are not fragile. Hebrews 13:8 says Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever.

Try this tonight

Step outside and look at the sky. Ask what each thing needed in order to exist. Then thank God that He depends on no one and that everyone who trusts in Christ can depend on Him. Keep the conversation open: “What else do you wonder about God?” A calm welcome to the next question teaches as much as this answer does.
